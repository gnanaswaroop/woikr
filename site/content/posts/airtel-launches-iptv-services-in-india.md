<p>Airtel has launched their IPTV services today in India under the banner of "<a href="http://airtel.in/interactive/index.html" target="_blank">digital TV Interactive</a>". Note that this service is different from the DTH service under the "<a href="http://www.airtel.in/digitaltv" target="_blank">digital TV</a>" banner.</p>
<p>According to wikipedia, <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPTV" target="_blank">IPTV</a> (Internet Protocol Television) is a system where a digital television service is delivered using Internet Protocol over a network infrastructure, which may include delivery by a broadband connection. Commonly referred to as Triple Play (voice, data, and video), here is a diagram explaining how this works :</p>

<a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/01/iptv_block_dg.jpg"><img class="alignnone size-full wp-image-2017" title="iptv_block_dg" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/01/iptv_block_dg.jpg" alt="iptv_block_dg" width="500" height="309" /></a>
<p><a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/01/iptv_block_dg.jpg"></a>Note that the TV channel data uses a separate PVC (private virtual circuit) from the set top box at your home to the ISP. The equipments at service provider's end multiplex the data packets with the TV channel packets and send them to your device. The set top box them pulls out the TV channel data, decodes the audio and video, and send it out through the different ports. The same can also be done using a computer software. This data stream uses approx 2.5 - 5 Mbps of bandwidth and is separate from the actual broadband connection stream. So watching TV will NOT affect your download or upload speed.</p>

<p>I am particularly happy with the following ports. They indicate and bring us one step closer to HD content transmission in India:</p>

<blockquote>
<p><strong>S/PDIF:</strong> This will enable high quality 5.1 dolby digital or DTS audio. Hook it up with your home theater equipment and you can experience 5.1 channel surround sound audio. Note that this is dependent on the content provider. None of the TV channels currently support this. Hopefully the video on demand movie service and future HD channels will support Dolby audio.</p>
<p><strong>S-Video:</strong> Most LCD and plasma TVs and some CRTs have this input. This can also be used to send the video to a computer.<strong>USB interface:</strong> Probably used to update the firmware. It would be cool if it can play audio/video files off a pen drive/HDD.</p>
<p><strong>Component output:</strong> Finally, this will do justice to the so called "DVD quality" transmission and will deliver HD resolutions when HD transmissions are started in India.</p>
<p><strong>Reserved interface: </strong>Dunno what it is. However, the shape resembles that of an HDMI port - too good to be true.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>The service is currently available in Delhi, Noida and Gurgaon only. Probably because Airtel has the most number of subscribers in these cities. Subscribers will enjoy:</p>

<blockquote>
<p><strong>Powerful Electronic Programming Guide (EPG):</strong><br>
This is a much more comprehensive guide than the one available over standard DTH services.

<p><strong>Video on Demand (VOD):</strong><br>
Watch movies truly on demand. This is NOT like the scheduled broadcast over DTH. This is true on demand service, whenever you want.

<p><strong>Interactive applications:</strong><br>
Purchase pizza and movie tickets on your TV with the press of a button on the remote. Complete visual interactive menu available on screen.

<p><strong>Time Shift TV:</strong><br>
Pause and rewind live TV. Network based Personal Video Recorder (PVR) allows you to watch the content according to your convenience. Over 10000 programs available every week to view at your convenience.

<p><strong>Parental Control:</strong><br>
Parents can lock VOD content to prevent younger children from accessing inappropriate content.</blockquote>
