<p>After the <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_4#Antenna" target="_blank">major problems with iPhone 4</a> that led to a lot of backlash, Apple has apparently redesigning the Antenna in it's devices again. They filed a patent in 2009 that was <a href="http://www.patentlyapple.com/patently-apple/2010/12/apple-invents-new-kind-of-logo-antenna-for-portables.html" target="_blank">recently discovered</a> and shows their intentions to hide the antenna behind the Apple logo.</p>
<p><a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/patent_apple_antenna.jpg"><img title="patent_apple_antenna" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/patent_apple_antenna-300x129.jpg" alt="" width="300" height="129" /></a></p>
<p>The patent mentions that the logo would be made of a dielectric material that allows for the transmission and reception of radio-frequency signals. The patent applies to Wi-Fi signals as well as GSM 850/900/1800/1900 MHz so it's expected to be seen in both the MacBooks and the iPhone/iPod. Interestingly the iPad already uses a variation of this design as shown by <a href="http://www.ifixit.com/Teardown/iPad-Teardown/2183/1" target="_blank">iFixit's teardown</a> and iMac 27" is using it as well.</p>
<p><a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/ipad_antenna.jpg"><img title="ipad_antenna" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/ipad_antenna-300x216.jpg" alt="" width="300" height="216" /></a></p>
<p>We are still not sure how it'll prevent the death-grip problem. Possibly the plastic logo will act as a shield between the hands and the antenna. We sure hope so.</p>
