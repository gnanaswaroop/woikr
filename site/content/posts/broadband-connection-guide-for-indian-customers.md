<p>With a plethora of ISPs now offering broadband services in India, it has become increasingly difficult for consumers to decide which one to go for. This guide takes into consideration the features offered by nationwide ISPs and their pros and cons to help you decide which one is right for you.</p>
<p>It will be easy if we get to know a few jargons first:</p>

<blockquote>
<h4>Broadband</h4>
As per TRAI regulations, <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband" target="_blank">broadband</a> is an always on dedicated internet connection with connection speed greater than or equal to 256 kbps. Anything lower is not to be classified as a broadband connection.
<h4>Speed</h4>
This is the speed at which you will be able to transfer data to/from your computer. This means that more the speed, faster the downloads and uploads.
<h4>kbps</h4>
<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_rate_units#kilobit_per_second" target="_blank">kilo bits per second</a> is a unit for measuring data transfer over a broadband connection. Note that kbps is different from KBPS (notice the caps). 8 kbps = 1 KBPS. So, if you go for a 256 kbps connection, you will get effective download speeds of 256/8 = 32 KBPS (Kilo bytes per second).
<h4>mbps</h4>
<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_rate_units#Megabit_per_second" target="_blank">mega bits per second</a> is also a unit for measuring data transfer. 1 mbps = 1024 kbps = 128 KBPS.
<h4>Always on (PPPOE)</h4>
<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPPOE" target="_blank">Point To Point Over Ethernet</a> is a protocol to establish point to point connection over an ethernet. Most DSL modems and wifi routers support this protocol allowing an always on broadband connection which is established within seconds after switching on the device. This is the recommended type of connection as it works well with most wifi routers and devices like Xbox, PS3, cellphones with wifi etc.
<h4>Browser based login</h4>
An alternate method of authentication where you have to login to the ISP's website before you can start surfing the internet or download any files. This is not supported by DSL modems or wifi routers. This is not recommended as it is cumbersome to use it with wifi routers. Additionally, there are issues if you use devices like Xbox, PS3 and cellphones with wifi.
<h4>Uptime</h4>
The %age of time the broadband service is up without any disruptions. Most ISPs claim 99% uptime but are usually not able to deliver that.</blockquote>

<p>In addition to wired DSL connections, Reliance and Tata Indicom provide wimax based broadband services in many places. <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WiMAX" target="_blank">Wimax</a> is a wifi like technology with range of a few kilometers compared to a few meters for wifi. In this setup, the ISP installs an antenna/dish equipment at your premises. This setup is fine as long as you get good signals.</p>

<p>As per the above two tables it is clear that <strong>Airtel</strong> is the best broadband service provider in India with awesome speed and excellent customer service. The only drawback we could find with it is that it is not available at a few locations even in big cities like Delhi &amp; NCR. <strong>BSNL</strong> comes next and scores with excellent speeds. Don't expect anything from the customer support though. <strong>Tata Indicom</strong> comes 3rd offering great uptime and average speed and customer service. Their main drawback is that it's not reliable in terms of speed and customer support. They will move up if they are a bit more consistent.</p>
<p>Clearly, <strong>Reliance</strong> sucks big time. With pathetic data transfer speeds coupled with old school browser based login, it is clearly the worst broadband service available. Many users have complained that most of the time Reliance's servers are overloaded and unable to authenticate. Stay away from it.</p>

<p>Go for <strong>BSNL </strong>only if Airtel is not available in your area.</p>
