</tbody></table> </div>  <p>As you can see, the plan gives you 24 Mbps (that’s 3 frickin’ MEGA bytes per second) at a monthly charge of 9999 INR. The plan also has a data transfer limit of 220 GB per month with NO night unlimited option, after which you will be charged at 20 paise per MB. Also, apparently, the “plan shall be offered after ascertaining the technical feasibility of the committed speed”, which basically means the plan will only be available to people living within a range of .5 – 1 km of a BSNL telephone exchange.</p>  <p>With these conditions and at such high monthly rental, the plan will&#160; probably only be used by:</p>  <ol>   <li>     <div>BSNL employees living close to a BSNL telephone exchange</div>   </li>    <li>     <div>Rich people living close to a BSNL telephone exchange</div>   </li> </ol>  <p></p>  <p>Although, I am not ruling out the fact that BSNL is only doing good for the country by launching this plan. At least someone has the balls to offer such speeds to consumers in India. 220 GB is a decent bandwidth quota, however, we will need something close to 500 GB to do justice to such speed. And just like every new piece of tech launched in India (no matter how long back it was launched in other countries), the price has been kept absurdly high.</p>  <p>BTW, did I mention that they are offering 1000 INR worth of free calls every month? Plus, they will throw in a free wifi modem (wow!) if you pay them advance rental for 12 months, which, hold your horses, comes out close to 1 lakh INR after discount.</p>  <p>As I said, completely absurd.</p>  <p>[via <a href="http://bsnl.co.in/service/dataone_tariff.htm" target="_blank">bsnl.co.in</a>]</p>
