<p>While wearing 3D glasses to watch immersive 3D videos in an IMAX movie theatre is fun, it's dorky if you need to do the same on your phone/tablet while you're on the move. <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostereoscopy" target="_blank">Glasses Free 3D</a> is the perfect (till we have 3D holograms) solution till then. It was introduced commercially on the recently released <a href="/gaming/nintendo-3ds-starts-pre-order-releases-price-details" target="_blank">Nintendo 3DS</a> pocket gaming console and is now demoed on the iPad2. </p>  <p><a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/Glasses-Free-3D-On-iPad.jpg"><img title="Glasses-Free-3D-On-iPad" alt="Glasses-Free-3D-On-iPad" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/Glasses-Free-3D-On-iPad_thumb.jpg" width="362" height="237" /></a></p>  <p></p><p></p>  <p><strong>How does Glasses Free 3D work?</strong>     <br />The device detects the user's viewing angle and generates a 3D illusion against the picture accordingly.&#160; Most games previously simulated the 3D effect used the gadget's accelerometer.</p>  <p>httpv://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bBQQEcfkHoE</p>  <p>It'll be uber-cool if some one wrote a processing engine to convert existing 3D videos to be rendered into &quot;Glasses Free 3D&quot; effect. Most computers have a webcam now anyway for the processing engine to detect the viewer's head (viewing angle), it can give 3D effect on regular monitors/LCD(s) too.</p>  <p>We sincerely hope that Apple incorporates this functionality in the upcoming iOS5. Now who wouldn't want to play <a href="/gaming/first-person-super-mario-bros-will-satisfy-you-fragaholics-video" target="_blank">Super Mario Bros 3D</a> using this?</p>  <p>[via <a href="http://www.macstories.net/news/ipad-2-head-tracking-glasses-free-3d/" target="_blank">MacStories.net</a>]</p>
