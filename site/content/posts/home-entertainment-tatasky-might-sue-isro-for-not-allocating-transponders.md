<p style="text-align: justify;">It looks like TataSky has exhausted all polite means to get critical transponders it needs to stay competitive in the DTH ecosystem in India. As reported earlier, they are already <a href="/home-entertainment/tatasky-is-maxed-out-on-its-capacity-to-beam-hd-channels/">maxed out on their capacity to beam HD channels</a> in India and are trying out various methods like vertical polarity, SAT CR etc. to figure out a way to increase their capacity of carrying HD channels in India. But the truth is, with millions of installations already in place, it is next to impossible to implement a solution that will work for everyone.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">TataSky’s CEO <a href="http://www.business-standard.com/article/management/we-might-have-to-go-legal-against-isro-harit-nagpal-113061801000_1.html">says</a> that they requested for 12 transponders on ISRO’s GSAT 10 in 2007 and should have been allocated the transponders by 2009. However, GSAT 10 launch was delayed by 3 years (!) and even after several months of the satellite launch, they have not yet received the transponders [<a href="http://www.business-standard.com/article/management/we-might-have-to-go-legal-against-isro-harit-nagpal-113061801000_1.html">source</a>].</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">This is a big goof up by state run ISRO and they are on the receiving end of the wrath of a lot of disappointed and irritated TataSky subscribers. We hope the issue is resolved soon and that TataSky can catch up to the other operators in the HD arena.</p>
