<p>So the big question is, how green are the gadgets that you use/used? When we're talking about green/eco-friendly, what we're looking for is</p>

<ul>
	<li>How high the emissions are during production and  gadget usage.</li>
	<li>What material the product is made of (are those eco-friendly?) And how minimal paper/plastic the gadget package comes with.</li>
	<li>Is your gadget energy efficient or Do you have to charge twice a day?</li>
</ul>
<p><img class="alignnone size-full wp-image-7749" title="iPhone-Earth" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/iPhone-Earth.jpg" alt="" width="215" height="323" /></p>
<p><br>
</p>
<p>GeekAPhone guys put together an interesting info-graphic which depicts how green the iPhone really is. Some of the noteworthy points are</p>

<ul>
	<li>iPhone parts come from 6 different countries and are assembled at China.</li>
	<li>2 million phones (not just iPhones) are dumped every week, that's huge. I wonder where this landfill is.</li>
	<li>Glass, Steel and battery make up 77% of the iPhone weight.</li>
	<li>iPhone4 reduced CO2 emissions by 18% when compared to it's precedent iPhone 3G</li>
	<li>Less than 10% of the iPhone(s) are recycled. In India most of them are either passed on to kids in the family or trashed, despite Apple offers free recycling of their products.</li>
	<li>iPhone emits 2 and 1/2 times less radiation than the level which can cause behavioural changes to animals, so the human race is safe for now. If you're using those cheap China mobiles - you're probably screwed.</li>
</ul>
