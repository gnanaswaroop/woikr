<p>OK. I have been wanting to write about this since the last few days, so I am gonna spill it out directly. Mobile numbers in India are now (also) starting with the digit “8”. <p>The <a href="http://www.dot.gov.in/numbering_plan/nnp2003.pdf" target="_blank">National Numbering Plan</a> on the DOT website hasn’t been updated with this yet and shows these numbers as “Reserved for future services”:</p>

<a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/10/image20.png"><img title="image" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/10/image_thumb17.png" alt="image" width="454" height="474" /></a>
<p>Apparently, some Vodafone customers are <a href="http://www.techbangalore.com/india-to-get-mobile-numbers-starting-with-8/" target="_blank">having problems</a> in calling these numbers. Looks like Vodafone needs to update its systems.</p>
<p>A lot of people still do not get it when you give a number starting with “800” to them. Understandable. However, I was really surprised when the customer care executives at Airtel Broadband asked me thrice to give me an alternate <strong>mobile</strong> number. I had to tell them that mobile numbers are now starting with “8” and that  this is an Airtel mobile number only. I still don’t think they believed me.</p>
<p>So don’t be surprised the next time you get a call from a number starting with “8” or your friend sends a message saying that he has a new number beginning with “8” (or, looking at the NNP table above, “7”). They are all just fancy new mobile numbers.</p>
<p>Oh, and yeah, since “8” is the new “9”, you can get a good choice of numbers in this scheme. I got 800-xxx-SETU for myself :).</p>
