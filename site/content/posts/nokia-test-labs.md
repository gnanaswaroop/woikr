I've always appreciated how durable those plasticky Nokia phones are. I appreciated that even more when I <a href="/news/get-your-iphone-repaired-in-noida" target="_blank">broke my iPhone</a> while playing iBaseball :)

<a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2008/11/keypad_pressing_test.jpg"><img class="size-medium wp-image-1786" title="keypad_pressing_test" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2008/11/keypad_pressing_test-300x199.jpg" alt="" width="300" height="199" /></a>

Now after watching the <a href="http://video.google.com/videoplay?docid=1683931603521695595&amp;hl=en" target="_blank">Nokia Labs tour video</a> by <a href="http://www.allaboutsymbian.com/" target="_blank">AAS</a> guys, I've become even more of a fan of the build quality of Nokia's phones.

A few facts to get you interested:
<ul>
	<li>On an average they break 2000 pieces to test each model.</li>
	<li>2000 different mechanical tests.</li>
	<li>Temperature range from -40° C to 85° C.</li>
	<li>Super slow motion cameras for drop tests. 100,000 frames/second!</li>
	<li>All keys/buttons/sliders are tested almost a million times.</li>
</ul>
You can see many more pictures from the labs at <a href="http://gizmodo.com/5094602/a-look-at-the-nokia-damage-test-labs" target="_blank">Gizmodo</a>. Make sure you watch their brilliant slow-motion drop test video too.
