<p style="text-align: justify;">At a typical social event or gathering where people come handy with their digital cameras, you may come across a comparative conversation quite similar to the following :</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="color: #000000;"><em>A: “ That’s a nice cam! How many Megapixels?”<br>
B: “8.0. And yours?”<br>
A: “Oh! I just sold my 7.1 one last month and bought a 10.0 MP one”<br>
B: “Impressive. I guess I will have to upgrade mine soon as well!”</em></span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">I am sure I will leave both “A” and “B’ wide-eyed if I tell them that a few camera manufacturers are talking today about 50 Megapixels ….and more! Leading the race of Camera Sensor resolution are two companies <a href="http://www.phaseone.com" target="_blank">PhaseOne</a> and <a href="http://www.hasselbladusa.com" target="_blank">Hasselblad</a> that are going to produce 60.5 and 50 Megapixels cameras respectively. These are unparalleled resolutions till date, and <span style="color: #ff6600;"><strong>woikr</strong></span> brings to you a brief on the two resolution monsters:</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong><span style="color: #ff6600;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;">The PhaseOne P65+</span></span></strong></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">July last month, Denmark based PhaseOne announced its latest model <strong>P65+</strong> which will provide a whooping <strong>60.5 Megapixels</strong> resolution. It utilises a new “Sensor+” design and is the world’s first full-frame capture system based on actual 645 film format.</p>
<p><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/p65.jpg" rel="lightbox[149]"><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-121" title="The P65+" src="/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/p65-300x287.jpg" alt="" width="300" height="287" srcset="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/p65-300x287.jpg 300w, /wp/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/p65.jpg 471w" sizes="(max-width: 300px) 100vw, 300px"></a></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">A 60.5 Megapixel shot means that you will be taking an image having 8984 x 6732 pixels. This in turn means a 180MB data file for one image (8-bit RGB). So, I suggest you better equip yourself with enough disk space if you plan to buy these! To facilitate such image files, the camera has a 1.3GB High Speed RAM built-in and is able to provide a decent 1 frame per second (fps) of image capturing speed. The P65+ also boasts of the largest live capture area ever with a dimension of 53.9mm x 40.4mm, truly justifying the term WYSIWYG. Its salient features are jotted here:</p>
<ul>
<li>60.5 Megapixels</li>
<li>Full frame sensor</li>
<li> 1 fps speed (or 1 capture per 1 second)</li>
<li> 16 bit image capture</li>
<li> 12.5 stops dynamic range</li>
<li> ISO 50-800</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong><span style="color: #ff6600;">The Hasselblad H3DII-50</span></strong></span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">The next leader in terms of megapixel count is the <strong>H3DII-50</strong> by Hasselblad. It offers a magnificent <strong>50 Megapixels</strong> resolution using Kodak’s 50 MP sensor which can produce a 6132×8176 pixels image (150MB TIFF file size). The Sensor measures 36×48mm, twice the physical size of the largest 35mm DSLR sensors.</p>
<p><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/hasselblad1.jpg" rel="lightbox[149]"><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-122" title="The H3DII-50" src="/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/hasselblad1-300x208.jpg" alt="" width="300" height="208" srcset="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/hasselblad1-300x208.jpg 300w, /wp/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/hasselblad1.jpg 682w" sizes="(max-width: 300px) 100vw, 300px"></a></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">The H3DII-50 offers some really unique features like the provision of two view-finders: you can either choose the view-finder at <em>eye-level</em> or <em>waist-level</em>! And when attached with a Global Image Locator (GIL) recording device, this camera can tag the captured images with GPS coordinates, time and altitude. So we are talking here about immediate geo-tagging of your photographs on Google Earth and Flickr Map applications when you upload the image.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/flickr-map-3.jpg" rel="lightbox[149]"><img class="alignnone size-full wp-image-166" title="flickr-map-3" src="/wp-content/uploads/2008/08/flickr-map-3.jpg" alt="" width="506" height="287"></a></p>
<p>The features of H3DII-50 are summarised here:</p>
<ul>
<li>50 Megapixels</li>
<li>Full frame sensor</li>
<li> 0.9 fps (or 1 capture per 1.1 second)</li>
<li> 16 bit image capture</li>
<li>12.5 stops dynamic range</li>
<li> ISO 50-400</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="color: #ff6600;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong><br>
“Are these for me?”</strong></span></span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Well I would say <span style="color: #000000;"><strong>Yes</strong>,</span> only if you are a top-notch full-fledged professional photographer and doing great in the photography market.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">And I won’t give you a penny to guess that the best available camera technologies will be coming to us at “some” price. The first introductory price for <strong>P65+</strong> Camera system is <strong>$41,990</strong> and the <strong>H3DII-50</strong> will be introduced for <strong>$39,995!</strong> So they are likely to burn a crater in your pocket, unless of course, your company sponsors these for you or you are someone who owns a company yourself! 🙂</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">The huge resolution that these cameras will offer will come handy mainly for shoots where there is absolutely no compromise on the image quality, captured colours and noise absence. Such resolutions are not required for day-to-day photography where 7-10 megapixels suffices. In fact, people prefer to shoot at low resolutions for  faster continuous mode shooting, lower file sizes and easy file-sharing. Thus, the manufactures of these cameras are no doubt targeting professionals and companies that are involved in shooting big commercial events like fashion shows, or say, the launch of a new car model.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="color: #ff6600;"><strong><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><br>
“I want to be the first to test these mean machines! Where can I find them?”</span></strong></span></p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Both PhaseOne and Hasselblad are planning to demonstrate the state-of-the-art <strong>“Sensor+” </strong>and <strong>“645 Sesnor”</strong> technologies through the trade show <a href="http://photokina.en.koelnmesse.info/" target="_blank">Photokina 2008</a>, to be held from 23rd to 28th Sept 2008 at Cologne, Germany. The cameras will be reaching the markets approximately early next year (2009).</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">So go ahead and surprise your friends with these facts the next time they brag about their 10MP cameras, and ask them to start thinking in Gigapixels now!</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Cheers!</p>
