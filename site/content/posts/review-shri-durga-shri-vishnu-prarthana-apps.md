<p>A few days ago <a href="http://www.robosoftin.com/" target="_blank">Robosoft</a> released <a href="/news/indian-deity-on-the-iphone/" target="_blank">Shri Durga &amp; Shri Vishnu <em>prarthana </em>apps</a> for the iPhone and iPod touch. The apps look pretty neat in the screenshots &amp; the video. But are they worth the price? Read on to find out.</p>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2529" title="img_0043" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0043-200x300.png" alt="img_0043" width="200" height="300" /> <a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0039.png"><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2525" title="img_0039" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0039-200x300.png" alt="img_0039" width="200" height="300" /></a><a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0039.png"></a>
<p><strong>Technical aspects:</strong></p>
<p>Starting with the nicely shaped icons to the awesome backgrounds, the apps follow an elegant and religious color code which is soothing to the eye. You can choose to start the apps in either <em>aarti </em>or <em>chalisa </em>mode. The interface is well designed and is easy to use.</p>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2526" title="img_0040" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0040-200x300.png" alt="img_0040" width="200" height="300" /> 
<p>Double tap anywhere and a pane appears at the bottom using which you can control the current activity and switch between <em>aarti </em>and <em>chalisa </em>modes. The settings button will take you to the Settings page which is pretty neat and intuitive:</p>

<a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0041.png"><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2527" title="img_0041" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0041-200x300.png" alt="img_0041" width="200" height="300" /></a>
<p>The Settings page lets you choose the starting mode for the app. You can also choose the language of display for <em>Shlokas </em>and their meanings. </p>

<a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0044.png"><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2530" title="img_0044" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0044-200x300.png" alt="img_0044" width="200" height="300" /> </a><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2533" title="img_0048" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0048-200x300.png" alt="img_0048" width="200" height="300" />
<p>In the <em>chalisa </em>mode, the chalisa plays in the background and the current <em>shloka </em>and its meaning is displayed on the screen alongwith an accompanying image.  The display changes automatically with each <em>shloka </em>of the <em>chalisa</em>. The common verse is played in between the <em>shlokas</em>.</p>

<a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0047.png"><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2532" title="img_0047" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0047-200x300.png" alt="img_0047" width="200" height="300" /> </a><a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0042.png"><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2528" title="img_0042" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0042-200x300.png" alt="img_0042" width="200" height="300" /></a>
<p>In the <em>aarti </em>mode, there are touch sensitive items on the screen. Touch the corresponding item to light the <em>diyas </em>and <em>aggarbattis </em>(incense sticks), offer flowers and apply <em>Tilak </em>to the deity. You can also touch the bells to ring them. You can then perform <em>aarti </em>by dragging the <em>aarti diya</em> around the screen. Don't worry, if you accidently leave it, it won't drop. It will levitate and slowly come back to the ground. Also, you cannot cover the deity with flowers. Care has been taken that the pile of flowers does not exceed a certain height. You can also shake the phone to ring the bells!</p>
<p>Currently, you will have to purchase a seperate app for each deity. Maybe with in app purchases API in the iPhone OS 3.0, they can offer a generic prarthana app with different deity specific extensions for sale inside the app.</p>
<p><strong>Religious aspects:</strong></p>
<p>Since I have limited knowledge about Hindu religious procedures, I asked a strong theist friend of mine to check out the apps. Here is what he had to say:</p>
<p>Both the apps are awesome and show accurate meaning of the <em>shlokas </em>and use ornamental language which does justice to the Sanskrit words. The music is very good. The photos in Shri Vishnu <em>prarthana</em> app are excellent.</p>
<p><a href="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0045.png"><img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-2531" title="img_0045" src="/wp/wp-content/uploads/2009/04/img_0045-200x300.png" alt="img_0045" width="200" height="300" /></a></p>
<p>He gave a big thumbs up for both the apps, however, he pointed out that photos in Shri Durga app are not a nice as Shri Vishnu app, and, apparently, do not match the playing verse sometimes.  Another thing that he pointed out was that, usually, <em>chalisa </em>is not recited with the common verse repeating in between the <em>shlokas</em>. These are minor deltas and can be fixed in a future release.</p>
<p><strong>Verdict:</strong></p>
<p>Both the apps do pretty well in technical as well as their religious aspects. They are a must have for people who are constantly on the move and want to offer prayers to the almighty. Also, superstitiously speaking, installing them might keep evil forces like thieves away from your iPhone ;). Moreover, its good to have your deity always with you.</p>
